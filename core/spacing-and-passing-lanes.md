# Spacing and Passing Lanes

Most of basketball happens in the empty space. Five players on each team, but the court is 94 by 50 feet. That's 4,700 square feet with ten bodies in it. The court is almost entirely open. The game lives in the openness, not in the bodies.

---

## Why Spacing Matters

Every coach says "space the floor." Stay spread out. Don't bunch up. But nobody explains WHY.

```
FIVE PLAYERS BUNCHED TOGETHER:
  The defense collapses into one area.
  No passing lanes. No driving lanes.
  One defender can guard two players.
  The court shrinks.

FIVE PLAYERS PROPERLY SPACED:
  The defense is stretched across the whole floor.
  Every passing lane is open.
  Every player is a threat.
  One defender can only guard one player.
  The court opens up.
```

Spacing isn't about where the players are. It's about the quality of the space between them. Five players in the right spots create a web — every cut has room to operate, every drive has a lane, every pass has a window. The defense can't take away everything because there's too much ground to cover.

---

## Passing Lanes

Great passers don't see players. They see lanes. The gap between two defenders. The window between the help man and the ball handler's defender. The opening that exists for half a second before it closes.

```
THE PASS DOESN'T GO:
  From player to player

THE PASS GOES:
  Through the gap between defenders
  Through the window that opens when the help steps up
  Through the lane that exists for one heartbeat
```

The greatest passers don't pass to where you are. They pass to where you're going to be. The ball arrives at the spot the same moment you do. That's not luck. That's a passer who sees the lane before it opens and trusts it'll be there.

---

## The Cut

A cut is a player attacking the open space. The ball-side defender is watching the ball. You take one hard step toward your man, then explode to the basket. For a half-second, you're in the gap — between your defender and the help — and the ball meets you there.

The best cuts aren't to a spot on the floor. They're THROUGH a gap in the defense. You read where the opening is and attack it. The defense was guarding spots. You moved through the space between spots.

```
BAD CUT:
  Predictable. Same speed. Same direction.
  The defender knows where you're going.
  No change of pace, no setup.

GOOD CUT:
  Set it up. Walk your man into position.
  Then explode — change speed, change direction.
  Hit the gap before the defense can rotate.
  The ball is already on its way.
```

---

## Off-Ball Movement Creates Everything

The ball handler gets all the attention. But four players are moving without the ball on every possession. Where they move — and when — creates every open shot, every driving lane, every mismatch the offense gets.

A player standing still is invisible to the offense and easy for the defense. A player who moves without the ball — screening, cutting, relocating — forces the defense to make choices. And every choice the defense makes opens something else up.

The best offenses aren't built around the guy with the ball. They're built around the four guys moving without it.

---

*The court is mostly empty space. The teams that use the space beat the teams that ignore it.*

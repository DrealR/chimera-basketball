# Three Things

Every moment on the court is one of three things.

```
┌──────────┐
│ OFFENSE  │  You have the ball
└────┬─────┘
     │
┌────┴──────────┐
│  TRANSITION   │  The moment between them
└────┬──────────┘
     │
┌────┴─────┐
│ DEFENSE  │  They have the ball
└──────────┘
```

Most coaches teach offense and defense separately. Run this play on offense. Guard your man on defense. Two separate things. Two separate drills. Two separate mindsets.

The great ones teach transition — the two to three seconds between losing the ball and getting back on defense. Or between grabbing the rebound and pushing the break. That chaotic space where the floor is wide open and everything is possible.

---

## Why Transition Wins Games

Watch any blowout. The team that's winning isn't running better plays. They're faster in transition. They turn defense into offense before the other team can set up. They turn a missed shot into a fast break before anyone reacts.

The 2-3 seconds of transition contain more basketball than the 20 seconds of halfcourt offense. Because in transition, nothing is organized. No one is in position. It's pure reading, pure reacting, pure basketball.

```
HALFCOURT OFFENSE:
  Structured. Predictable. Coach's system.
  Players are in ROLES.

TRANSITION:
  Unstructured. Chaotic. Player's instinct.
  Players are THEMSELVES.
```

Who you are in transition is who you really are as a player. The rest is just following instructions.

---

## The Cone Drill

There's a drill — three cones, one ball. Behind the back at cone one. Between the legs at cone two. Spin at cone three. Drive to the basket.

```
Cone 1 → Behind the back. Change direction.
Cone 2 → Between the legs. Stay low, stay tight.
Cone 3 → Spin. Use their momentum against them.
DRIVE  → Attack the basket.
```

The drill isn't three separate moves. It's one sequence — change direction, change pace, change angle, go. When a player does it right, you can hear the rhythm. When they do it wrong, you hear hesitation between the cones. The hesitation is where the drill breaks. The flow is where the drill lives.

---

*The offense-defense split is how beginners see basketball. The real game lives in the transition between them.*

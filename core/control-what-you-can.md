# Control What You Can

You can't control the refs. You can't control whether the shot goes in. You can't control what the other team does.

So what CAN you control?

---

## Start With the Small Stuff

A kid shows up to practice stressed about the big game on Friday. They're thinking about the other team's center, the crowd, whether scouts are coming.

Stop. Forget Friday.

Can you control how the ball feels in your hand right now? Yes. Feel the seams. Bounce it twice. Feel the rhythm.

Can you control your footwork on this one move? Yes. Load. Fake. Go.

Can you control your composure when you get fouled? Yes. Walk to the line. Same routine. Same tempo.

That's where it starts. Not with the season. Not with the game plan. With your hands and your feet and your focus.

---

## What You Actually Control

```
YOUR COMPOSURE:
  Whether you stay calm after a bad call.
  Whether you let a turnover live in one possession or five.
  Complete control. Always.

YOUR EFFORT:
  How hard you sprint back on defense.
  How physical you are on screens.
  How focused you are in the huddle.
  Complete control. Always.

YOUR PREPARATION:
  Did you put the reps in?
  Do your hands know where the ball goes without looking?
  Did you watch film?
  Complete control. Before the game.

YOUR DECISIONS:
  Pull up or drive? Pass or shoot?
  You can't control the outcome.
  You CAN control whether you made the right read.
```

---

## What You Don't Control

The refs. The crowd. Whether your shot goes in. Whether the other team hits a buzzer-beater. Whether the coach plays you. Whether the ball bounces your way.

Coaches who teach kids to focus on outcomes set them up for frustration. The kid who measures success by wins and losses has a bad time when the other team is just better. The kid who measures success by effort, composure, and decision-making can have a great game in a loss.

---

## How This Plays Out

Watch two players react to the same missed shot:

```
PLAYER A:
  Misses the three.
  Drops their head.
  Jogs back on defense.
  Still thinking about the miss on the next possession.
  Misses again because they're tight.
  One miss became three.

PLAYER B:
  Misses the three.
  Sprints back on defense.
  Gets a stop.
  Catches the ball on the next possession, same confidence.
  Drives instead of shooting. Draws the foul.
  One miss stayed one miss.
```

The difference isn't talent. Player B controls what they can — their effort, their composure, their next decision. Player A is trying to control what they can't — whether the shot goes in.

---

## The Coaching Move

Don't tell a kid "we need to win this game." Tell them:

"Sprint back every time. Make the right read every possession. Stay calm when it gets loud."

Those are things they can actually DO. Winning is what happens when enough people do those things. But you don't control winning. You control the things that lead to it.

---

*You can't control the scoreboard. You can control your effort, your composure, and your decisions. Start there. The rest follows.*
